How it works. Winds and temperatures come from the NOAA GFS and ECMWF models through Open-Meteo (850–150 hPa, about 5,000–44,600 ft), taken at each point at its estimated time overhead and interpolated between levels. Each level is scored over the whole trip: climb (with climb winds), cruise and descent. Cruise fuel follows the weight as fuel burns, and the off-OPT penalty is measured from the OPT for the current weight. The "4 wind levels" are the set whose straight-line interpolation, which is what the FMS does, best matches the forecast profile around the levels that matter.

Limits. Planning cross-check, not certified performance data, and not an approved EFB application. The FMS, OFP, ATC and REC MAX/buffet margins always govern.
